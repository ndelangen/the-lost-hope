# AD&D 2nd Edition Monstrous Manual centipede source check

The checked copy of TSR’s *Monstrous Manual*, product 2140, is the January 1994 third printing of the 1993 edition. Printed p. 42, viewer page 45, contains all four columns, their prose and the illustration. The page image has SHA-256 `a49aaeb540021a5f6e8d6378ba79ab32c0e33a68bd74816eb1895ea60383cbc6`. The reflowed electronic edition agrees and spreads the material across pp. 139–141. All four are carnivores, active at any time and neutral, with one attack and no listed special defense or magic resistance. The centipedes use poison and the Tunnel Worm uses lunging. The larger-sounding Huge name is the smallest form in this particular table.

## Giant Centipede

- Any terrain, common, organization nil, Non- (0) intelligence, treasure nil, 2–24 appearing. AC 9, movement 15, **2 hp** in the Hit Dice row, THAC0 20, bite damage nil, Tiny (1 ft.), morale unsteady (5–7), 35 XP.
- A bitten victim gets +4 to the save against paralysis for 2d6 hours. The centipede itself takes −1 to all its saving throws. It hunts from camouflage, dropping on prey or coming out of hiding. Individuals fight independently even in groups and can fight over fallen prey. The prose gives a roaming carnivore that favors small mammals and may resort to cannibalism.
- Its long, many-legged body has chitinous plates and undulates as it moves. Rock-dwelling specimens are gray, underground ones black, and woodland ones brown or red. The prose says over one foot long, while the table uses 1 ft. as the size figure.

## Huge Centipede

- Any terrain, common, organization nil, Non- (0) intelligence, treasure nil, 5–30 appearing. AC 9, movement 21, **1 hp** in the Hit Dice row, THAC0 20, bite damage nil, Tiny (6 in.), morale unsteady (5–7), 35 XP.
- Its paragraph treats the Huge as identical to the Giant except where stated. On a failed victim save its weaker poison immobilizes for 1d6 hours. The victim’s +4 save bonus is inherited from the Giant description by that reference and is not restated. The Huge’s own saves receive −2 instead of the Giant’s −1. Its prey includes mice and large insects, and Giant Centipedes prey on it. The adult proportions and habitat-related colors can follow the linked Giant description at the smaller scale.

## Megalo-centipede

- Any terrain, very rare, organization nil, Animal (1) intelligence, treasure nil, 1–4 appearing. AC 5, movement 18, HD 3, THAC0 17, bite 1–3, Medium (5 ft.), morale average (8–10), 175 XP.
- Victims get no bonus against the bite’s acidic poison. Failure means death, while success still causes 2d4 acid damage. The table’s 1–3 bite damage is a separate value. The creature takes no penalty to its own saves. It hunts more cunningly than the smaller centipedes, still without cooperating. It takes deer-sized animals in the wild and human-sized or smaller prey underground. Its paragraph gives no distinct palette.

## Tunnel Worm

- Subterranean, rare, swarm organization, Non- (0) intelligence, treasure (M, N, Q), 1–6 appearing. AC 4, movement 6, HD 9+3, THAC0 11, tabular damage **2–8**, Gargantuan (25 ft. or longer), morale steady (12), 1,400 XP.
- It lunges from a hidden burrow at +2 to attack. A hit seizes the target in its mandibles without immediate damage. The prose specifies one round to chew through leather or worse, two rounds through better than leather up to chain mail, and three through stronger armor. After breaching the armor it automatically deals **2d8 each round**. Keep the printed 2–8 row visible with the explicit 2d8 procedure. The book supplies no additional attack case for the tabular range.
- It drops the target and retreats after suffering at least 15 fire damage or losing 60% of its hit points. The text does not specify whether fire damage must come from a single event. It scavenges and lays eggs in carrion. Its lair can contain victims’ belongings. The paragraph mentions mandibles but gives no form-specific color or further anatomy.

## Illustration and catalog

The signed artwork depicts a reddish-brown, long-legged segmented centipede curled above a sword, with prominent antennae and elongated rear appendages. Tony DiTerlizzi’s color-art checklist explicitly identifies the assignment as “Centipede, Giant,” and he also names the Giant Centipede in his retrospective. The illustration sits under Tunnel Worm’s prose, but the artist’s records identify the depicted form as Giant Centipede. Their placement under Centipede and the Tunnel Worm’s stated relationship to the Giant support treating the four as beasts in the cross-edition catalog.
