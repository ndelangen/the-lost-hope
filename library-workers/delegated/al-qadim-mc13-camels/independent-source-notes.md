# MC13 Al-Qadim camels source review

*Monstrous Compendium Al-Qadim Appendix* is TSR product 2129, ISBN 1-56076-370-1, ©1992, for AD&D 2e. The credits name Wolfgang Baur and Steve Kurtz for design, C. Terry Phillips for editing, Jeff Easley for cover art, and Tom Baxa and Mark Nelson for interior art. The original loose leaves have no printed page numbers. In the inspected 74-page digital copy, the credits are viewer p. 3, the “Camel” leaf is viewer p. 11, and the Compiled Statistics Table is viewer p. 65. The Camel leaf includes no individual camel illustration or artist credit. [Credits][credits] · [Camel leaf][camel] · [Archived edition text][archive]

## Printed profiles

All four columns list AC 7, Large (8 ft. tall), Animal to Semi intelligence (1–4), and Nil for alignment, treasure, special defenses, and magic resistance. All list spitting and trample, and describe common diurnal herbivores in herds on any land. The separate descriptions give typical habitats and uses. The four columns give HD without fixed encounter HP. The appendix’s general rule uses d8s for HD unless specified otherwise. [Original Camel leaf][camel] · [Appendix stat conventions][conventions]

The leaf explicitly measures carrying capacity in gold pieces. It says camels are loaded kneeling and often refuse to rise when loaded above the encumbered figure. The same normal, encumbered, and fully encumbered numbers for Desert and War Camels appear in the later *Caravans* Campaign Guide p. 25 (p. 27), where the header explicitly says pounds. ## Shared handling and combat

- An ill-tempered camel has a 50% chance to spit when someone tries to ride it or use it as a pack animal. The victim then has a 25% chance of being blinded for 1–3 rounds. The entry gives no range, separate attack roll, or save for this handling event.
- A frightened herd has a 25% chance to stampede. For each exposed creature in its path, 2d4 camels trample that creature for 1–4 damage per camel. An affected creature stays down until the herd has passed.
- War Camels also have two 1–6 hoof attacks as part of their three normal attacks. The text says a successful hoof attack keeps the target prone. It gives no separate knockdown roll or duration. The ordinary table lists the other camels’ single attack by damage without a specific attack name.
- The leaf calls for experienced handling and refers to horse quality grades in the *Dungeon Master’s Guide* p. 36. Mountain Camels serve as pack animals. The source gives no fifth draft type, dedicated draft stat block, or camel-specific control check. [Original Camel leaf][camel]

## Form and habitat

The Desert Camel is a steady heavy-load animal for desert travel, with one hump, wide padded feet and a rolling gait. The Mountain Camel is a shaggy, two-humped pack animal of hills and mountains. Its combat figures match the Desert Camel, while its carrying values and described terrain differ. The Racing Camel is one-humped with small hooves and long slender legs. It is aggressive, spits even at people accustomed to handling it, and is usually trained to accept few riders. The one-humped War Camel is bred or trained to withstand combat and uses its bite and hooves. The general description gives coats from white through light tan to deep brown. [Original Camel leaf][camel]

## Summary discrepancies and catalog check

The Compiled Statistics Table gives THAC0 16 for Desert, Mountain, and Racing Camels, lists 1–12 Racing Camels, and drops the War Camel’s bite by listing only two attacks at 1–6 each. The dedicated Camel leaf gives 17, 17, and 19 respectively, lists 1–6 Racing Camels, and prints all three War Camel attacks. The War Camel’s description and the later *Caravans* reprint confirm three attacks. Use the dedicated MC13 leaf for all four profiles. The primary catalog contains generic and cavalry camels from other publications. No exact name and publication matches appeared for these four.
