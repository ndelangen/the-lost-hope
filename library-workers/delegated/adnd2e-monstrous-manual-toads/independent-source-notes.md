# AD&D 2nd Edition Monstrous Manual giant toad source check

TSR first published *Monstrous Manual*, product 2140, in 1993. The checked scan’s credits page identifies it as the January 1994 third printing. Printed p. 345, viewer page 348, contains the complete *Toad, Giant* table, all four descriptions and the family art. Its image SHA-256 is `009aab7ea9028e04a9aa2d5d7f757f4989b4aa25dc9b2d426567a9c148aca431`. The electronic edition spreads the same rules across pp. 945–947. All four are carnivores with colony organization, one attack, special attacks referred to the prose and nil magic resistance. All four table columns give nil special defenses. The Fire prose supplies conditional damage modifiers. The shared combat paragraph allows a one-round leap up to movement distance, clearing obstacles up to one-third of its length, with an attack during or at the end of the leap. A toad that lies motionless and then leaps at prey imposes −3 on the prey’s surprise roll. This is an ambush condition. The family’s dry, rough, warty skin varies from weak brown to iron red.

## Giant Toad

- Non-arctic or subterranean, common, any activity, Animal (1), no treasure, neutral, 1–12 appearing. AC 6, movement 6 and hop 6, HD 2+4, THAC0 17, damage 2–8, Medium (5 ft. long), morale unsteady (7), 120 XP.
- The entry describes giant toads near temperate water and in damp underground places, where they hunt. They may also live in cooler or drier places and raid livestock or city sewers. The general profile supplies no swallow, tongue or separate grappling procedure.

## Fire Toad

- Non-arctic or subterranean, rare, any activity, Low (5–7) intelligence, treasure C, chaotic neutral, 1–6 appearing. AC 10, movement 6 and hop 6, HD 4+1, THAC0 17, variable damage, Small (4 ft. long), morale average (8–10), 270 XP.
- Its only normal attack is a fireball exhaled up to 30 feet with a 5-ft. blast radius. Damage is 2–16, halved by a successful save vs. spell. It normally attacks when threatened, disturbed or defending its lair.
- The prose subtracts 1 per damage die from incoming fire attacks and adds 1 per die to incoming cold or water attacks. The table’s special-defense field still says nil. A liquid thrown at it provokes retreat, and it immediately breathes twice at the assailant during that same round. This is an exception to the ordinary one-attack entry.
- The text describes bright red skin with rough purple warts and a preference for dry surroundings.

## Ice Toad

- Arctic or subarctic land or subterranean, rare, day active, Average (8–10) intelligence, treasure Q, (C), neutral, 1–4 appearing. AC 4, movement 9 and hop 9, HD 5, THAC0 15, bite damage 3–12, Large (8 ft. long), morale steady (11–12), 270 XP.
- In addition to its bite it radiates cold within 10 feet of itself once every other round. The rules name “noncold-using creatures” as affected. They take 3–18 damage, halved by a successful save vs. spell. The entry does not define that category further.
- It speaks its own language and collects gems and jewelry, especially diamonds. It lives in cold climates and deep below ground. Its subsection supplies no distinct skin color, icy growths or other external markers.

## Poisonous Toad

- Non-arctic or subterranean, uncommon, any activity, Animal (1), no treasure, neutral, 1–8 appearing. AC 7, movement 6 and hop 6, HD 2, THAC0 19, bite damage 2–5, Medium (5 ft. long), morale unsteady (7), 175 XP.
- Its bite adds a save vs. poison with a +2 bonus. Failure puts the victim into a coma and causes death within 24 hours unless treated. The entry gives no treatment procedure or separate effect on a successful save.
- The book explicitly says it is visually indistinguishable from the common Giant Toad. Color variation is possible within that ordinary palette. A distinctive poison color or markings would contradict the description.

## Illustration and catalog

A red-magenta toad with dark purple warts, a yellow eye and a crouched profile appears below the Poisonous Toad paragraph, with no caption. The color pattern strongly matches the Fire Toad description. It does not identify the Poisonous Toad or establish an Ice palette. No legible signature or figure-specific credit identifies the artist.
