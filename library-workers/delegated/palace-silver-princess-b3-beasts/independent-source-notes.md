# B3 Palace of the Silver Princess beast source review

Jean Wells designed the original orange-cover B3 for the Dungeons & Dragons Basic rules, character levels 1–3. The title leaf credits TSR Hobbies, Inc. ©1981 and gives product 9044 and ISBN 0-935696-31-8. The 36-page Pandius scan is byte-for-byte identical to the local reference (SHA-256 `9bc609b00752ccf7a5c04250d9982b42117f8d0b1a59dfa586ce731cddac6e42`). The title and copyright leaf is p. 3. John D. Rateliff’s archived Wizards reissue page identifies this as Wells’s original and links the old ZIP. [Original orange-cover ][orange] · [Wizards reissue page][wizards]

The later green-cover revision credits Tom Moldvay and Jean Wells, also dated 1981. I checked the revised text for both creature names and found neither. The two profiles below come from Part 6, “New Monster Section,” of the orange-cover module. Neither name appeared in the primary bestiary catalog or portrait index. ## Giant Marble Snake

- The exact heading is “Giant Marble Snake” on printed p. 27 (p. 29). AC 8, HD 3, movement 150 ft. (50 ft.), 1 attack plus special, damage 1–6, 1–4 appearing, saves as a 3rd-level fighter (F3), morale 8, treasure type C, neutral. The rules describe a nonvenomous bite and give no generic fixed HP or XP value.
- An intended victim who fails a save versus Spell is charmed by the whistle and drawn toward the snake. The snake breaks its hold on that victim when it successfully strikes them. The block gives no range or fixed duration. The ballroom encounter, Room 29 on printed p. 25 (p. 27), sets the individual at 16 HP and calls for a save from everyone who hears the whistle. Its boxed description calls the hanging bell brass, while the encounter text describes the snake as hiding in a wire bell.
- Milk-white scales, gold facial hair like a lion’s mane, two long fangs, multifaceted eyes that show swirls of color in bright light, and translucent skin patches showing fine veins. It favors high sunny elevations, ledges, and old ruins, where it burrows in loose stone and dirt. The artwork on the stat page depicts the neighboring Diger entry’s pool. ## Monkey, Giant Marmoset

- The printed heading is “Monkey, Giant Marmoset” on p. 28 (p. 30). AC 5, HD 3, movement 180 ft. (60 ft.), 4 attacks, damage 1–6/1–6/1–8/1–4, 2–8 appearing, saves as F3, morale 8, treasure type J, neutral. The source gives no fixed HP or XP value.
- Ground fighting can include the furry tail spike for 1–4 damage. In trees the marmoset hangs from its tail and fights with bite and claws. The prose mentions dropping or throwing large stones down from cliffs without supplying a separate attack roll, damage, or range.
- The Entrance Level room 32, Cell D encounter on printed p. 16 (p. 18) has two Chaotic marmosets with 10 HP each. Both attack intruders except Travis. The room gives an upright height of 8 feet and 12-foot tails with sharp furry spikes. [Cell D encounter][cell]
- The marmosets roam jungles in family groups. The source says a young one can be trained as a guard and some grow large enough to be ridden by a halfling. A black-and-white drawing beside the entry shows long curled tails and dark facial markings. It is uncaptioned and gives no individual artist credit or palette. Printed p. 25 credits Jeff Dee, David S. La Force, Erol Otus, Jim Roslof, Laura Roslof, Stephen D. Sullivan, Jean Wells, and Bill Willingham as module artists.
