# Oriental Adventures Korobokuru and Spirit Folk source check

James Wyatt’s *Oriental Adventures* is the 2001 Wizards of the Coast D&D 3.0 book, ISBN 0-7869-2015-7. The credits page gives product code 620-T12015-001-EN. In the 256-page book scan, printed folios match page numbers across these entries. The publisher’s 13-page preview starts at printed p. 2 and ends at p. 14. It includes the complete Korobokuru entry and the first page of Spirit Folk. The book marks both species as absent from its featured Rokugan setting. ## Korobokuru

- **Character rules.** +2 Constitution, −2 Intelligence, Small, base speed 20 feet and black-and-white darkvision to 60 feet. Small size gives +1 to AC and attack rolls, +4 to Hide, smaller weapons and three-quarters of the Medium lifting and carrying limits. Racial bonuses are +2 on saves against poison, +2 on saves against spells and spell-like effects, +1 on attacks against goblins, hobgoblins, bugbears, bakemono and goblin rats, and +2 on Wilderness Lore. The +4 dodge bonus against giants is lost when the character loses their positive Dexterity bonus to AC, such as when flat-footed. Automatic languages are Common and Dwarven. Bonus languages are Giant, Goblin, Hengeyokai and Sylvan. Favored class is Barbarian, which is ignored when determining a multiclass XP penalty. [Printed p. 12][p12] · [Printed p. 13][p13]
- **Appearance.** Dwarflike, lean for their build, about 4 feet tall and 120–140 pounds. They have proportionately long limbs and are usually bowlegged. The text describes bright blue, green or brown eyes, small pointed ears, a rounded nose with flared nostrils, full lips, and thick, commonly blond or light-brown hair. Most adult men have sparse beards. Some women have short chin whiskers. The entry describes a cotton shirt and trousers or a loose kimono with a rope sash, earth tones, a stone pendant on leather and occasional flowers in the hair. [Printed p. 12][p12]
- **Communities and names.** The entry begins with secluded farms and villages, then gives examples including mountain valleys, tropical forests, snowy wooded mountainsides and volcanic crater lakes. Villages often consist of one extended family. The book describes campfire storytelling, small arts and a practice of honoring nearby nature spirits. The spoken Dwarven dialect lacks its own script in the description. Literate speakers use the Common script, and the racial language rules still list Common and Dwarven. A clan elder bestows a name that often refers to a natural feature. Printed examples are Bun, Bod, Dath, Fek, Mog, Tod and Vun for men, and Bin, Dim, Fain, Gim, Mem, Mon, Tas and Wan for women. Clan examples are Kuo-ban, Gia-mun, Hua-kag, Jun-tua, Ten-min and Yak-rui. [Printed p. 11][p11] · [Printed p. 12][p12] · [Printed p. 13][p13]

## Spirit Folk

The book uses three lineages within a shared playable identity. It describes human descendants of nature spirits who often live among humans near old bamboo groves, rivers and streams or deep ocean waters. They may share human community life and honor human ancestors and spirit ancestors. The book describes a general tendency toward neutral alignments. They usually have human names, with given names sometimes reflecting ancestry. The entry offers no separate list of personal names. [Printed p. 14][p14]

- **Shared rules.** No ability adjustment in Table 1–1. Medium-size with a 30-foot base speed and low-light vision, which retains color and detail and reaches twice as far as human vision in poor light. The spirit subtype makes them susceptible to spirit-targeting spells such as *protection from spirits* and *invisibility to spirits*. Their humanoid ancestry also leaves them susceptible to spells such as *hold person* and *charm person*. Automatic languages are Common and Spirit Tongue. Bonus languages are Aquan, Giant, Goblin, Hengeyokai and Nezumi. Favored class is Any, with their highest-level class ignored for the multiclass XP penalty. [Ability-adjustment table][p7] · [Printed p. 14][p14]
- **Bamboo.** +2 racial bonus on Wilderness Lore, +4 racial bonus on Hide in woods or forest and Trackless Step in natural surroundings. +2 racial bonus on saves against spells and spell-like effects with “earth,” “rock,” “stone” or “wood” in their names, earth-element shugenja spells, and wu jen spells connected to earth or wood. Innate *speak with animals* once per day works with any animal and lasts 1 minute at caster level 1 regardless of actual level. [Printed p. 14][p14] · [Printed p. 15][p15]
- **River and Sea swimming.** Both can breathe air and water and have a 30-foot swim speed. Routine swimming takes no Swim check. Special actions and hazards receive +8 on the check, and they may always choose to take 10 even if rushed or threatened. They may run while swimming in a straight line. [Printed p. 15][p15]
- **River.** +2 racial bonus on saves against spells and spell-like effects with “water” in the name, water-element shugenja spells and wu jen spells connected to water. Innate *speak with animals* works with any fish once per day for 1 minute at caster level 1 regardless of actual level. [Printed p. 15][p15]
- **Sea.** +2 racial bonus on saves against spells and spell-like effects bearing the fire descriptor. A DC 15 Knowledge (nature) check or untrained Intelligence check, with a +2 racial bonus, predicts the next 24 hours of weather. [Printed p. 15][p15]

## Art and encounter references

The grayscale lineup on printed p. 8 shows a labeled Korobokuru Male (4 feet 2 inches) and Spirit Folk Female (5 feet 2 inches). The Spirit Folk portrait is unassigned to a lineage. Shared prose describes a human appearance with slender eyes, a small mouth, thin eyebrows, very pale or golden skin and thick head hair, without facial or body hair. The lineup gives no figure-specific artist credit or visible signature. The credits list Raven Mimura as cover artist and name a separate roster of interior artists. [Printed p. 8][p8] · [Publisher preview, p. 7][preview-art] · [Printed p. 14][p14] · [Print credits][credits]

- **Korobokuru warrior.** Printed pp. 171–172 supply a first-level warrior, CR 1/2, Small Humanoid (Dwarf), 5 HP, AC 15 and speed 20 feet. Its text prints darkvision 120 feet and specifies Fortitude saves versus poison and Will saves versus spells and spell-like abilities. The playable text says 60 feet and does not name the save categories for its racial bonuses. The warrior’s habitat row says warm mountains and its prose includes jungles. Use the full chapter 1 habitat for the playable species. The picture on p. 171 is labeled Ki-rin. [Printed p. 171][p171] · [Printed p. 172][p172] · [Playable traits][p12]
- **Spirit Folk warrior.** Printed pp. 192–193 supply one first-level warrior block for all three lineages, CR 1/2, Medium-Size Humanoid (Spirit), 4 HP, AC 14, speed 30 feet and swim 30 feet for River and Sea. The habitat row gives temperate and warm forests for Bamboo and aquatic territory for River and Sea. Its usual alignments are lawful good for Bamboo, neutral good for River and chaotic good for Sea. The chapter 1 playable chapter gives a general neutral tendency. The Sea weather text on p. 193 omits “untrained” before Intelligence check, which is present on p. 15.
